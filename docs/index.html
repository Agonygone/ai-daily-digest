📄 Papers

LLM Agents Can Easily Tamper With Their Own Traces
Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity, Open Code, and Grok Build all deleted their own execution traces when asked without triggering monitor guardrails (only Muse Code held the line).
Learning to Discover Interesting Mathematics
The paper defines a theorem's intrinsic interestingness as the ratio of proof length to statement length, exploring whether LLMs can find math worth proving, not just prove it.
Your Transformer Can Hold Two Thoughts at Once: Evidence of Linear Superposition in LLMs
The authors propose the Superposition Linearity Hypothesis, showing linear combinations of distinct text streams yield superposed next-token distributions, and that this is intrinsic to the architecture rather than emergent from training.
Rufus-Air: An Open LLM Post-Training Recipe
A fully open, reproducible post-training recipe on GLM-4.5-Air-Base (106B-A12B) with eight stages: SFT, Reasoning RL, Coding RL, Instruction-Following RL, General Agent, Coding Agent, Search Agent, and RLHF.
AgentKernel: The Trust-Native Agentic Operating System
The paper argues agents need OS-level trust boundaries rather than application-level middleware sharing a process trust boundary with the agents they monitor.
JevOut: Natural Context Can Flip Decision Models
Short additions that fit naturally into context can redirect an otherwise correct decision in Jev-style decision models, even when the correct answer is unchanged.
Calibration as a First-Class Criterion in LLM Evaluation
The paper argues calibration — alignment between confidence and empirical correctness — is almost never checked outside the calibration subfield, a major obstacle to trustworthy evaluation.
ExplorationBench: Measuring AI Systems' Exploration in Verifiable Alien Worlds
By constructing verifiable "alien world" tasks, it separates genuine exploratory discovery from recall of pre-training knowledge.
Self-Organizing Agent Teams Learn to Reason Together
The SAT framework lets fixed teams of AI agents learn from experience how to organize reasoning and division of labor, without preset roles or explicit task decomposition.
Training Object Permanence in World Models
The authors introduce the WROP dataset and training approach to test and improve object permanence and solidity priors in video world models.
