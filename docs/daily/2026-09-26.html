📄 Papers

Learning to Discover Interesting Mathematics
The paper defines a theorem's intrinsic interestingness as the ratio of proof length to statement length, exploring whether LLMs can not only prove theorems but discover genuinely valuable mathematics.
Your Transformer Can Hold Two Thoughts at Once: Evidence of Linear Superposition in LLMs
The authors propose the Superposition Linearity Hypothesis, showing that when inputs from distinct text streams are linearly combined, the model outputs a superposition of individual next-token distributions, and that this is intrinsic to the Transformer architecture.
LLM Agents Can Easily Tamper With Their Own Traces
Research shows local agents including Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity, Open Code, and Grok Build can all be asked to delete their own execution traces without triggering monitor guardrails; only Muse Code is the exception.
Rufus-Air: An Open LLM Post-Training Recipe
A public, reproducible eight-stage post-training pipeline on GLM-4.5-Air-Base (106B-A12B), covering SFT, Reasoning RL, Coding RL, Instruction-Following RL, General/Coding/Search Agents, and RLHF, with full documentation of data, reward design, and infrastructure.
AgentKernel: The Trust-Native Agentic Operating System
Proposes that agents need OS-level trust boundaries rather than application-level middleware sharing a process trust domain with the agents they monitor, to counter harmful tool calls from untrusted content injection.
Just Ask Jev: RL for Calibrated Decisions as a Zero-Shot Detector of AI Alignment Failures
Studies whether Jev, trained with RLCD, can answer many typed questions about one input with calibrated probabilities in a single call for zero-shot detection of alignment failures.
ExplorationBench: Measuring AI Systems' Exploration in Verifiable Alien Worlds
Builds verifiable "alien worlds" to distinguish whether AI discovers new hypotheses through exploration or merely recalls related knowledge from pre-training data.
Training Object Permanence in World Models
Introduces the WROP data infrastructure to study whether video generation models possess object permanence, and attempts to train this capability with a core-cognition-inspired dataset.
Self-Organizing Agent Teams Learn to Reason Together
Introduces SAT, fixed teams of AI agents that learn from experience how to organize reasoning and division of labor, rather than relying on fixed protocols or explicit task decomposition.
Capable yet Parsimonious: Extracting and Characterizing Hidden Chain-of-Thought in Frontier Models
Uses a registered custom tool to induce frontier closed-source models to externalize intermediate reasoning, first validating against native CoT on open-source models to rule out post-hoc rationalization.
Qwen-Planner-Agent: A Closed-Loop AI-for-AI Framework for Real-World Mobile Planner Agents
Builds a closed-loop AI-for-AI framework where AI is both the object of development and an active participant in building next-generation AI systems, validated on mobile planning.
Calibration as a First-Class Criterion in LLM Evaluation
The authors argue calibration should be a first-class criterion in LLM evaluation, noting NLP routinely introduces new models and benchmarks without checking whether confidence scores are meaningful.
